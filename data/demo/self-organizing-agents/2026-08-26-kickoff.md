# Kickoff — Self-Organizing Agent Teams

Date: 2026-08-26 (Wed) 14:00–15:30
Attendees: Prof. Han, Mina, Leo, Jae
Note-taker: Jae

## Context
- Prof. Han: Most multi-agent LLM systems still use hand-designed roles and a fixed communication graph. The question for this semester: can a team of LLM agents organize itself for the task at hand?
- Target venue: ICML 2027 (deadline late January).

## Discussion
- Prof. Han: Fixed structures look fine in papers but break as soon as the task distribution shifts.
- Mina: Agreed. In my last project the planner-coder-tester pipeline collapsed on tasks that needed two coders.
- Leo: Same experience. Hand-designed topologies are brittle — we all seem to agree on that.
- Mina: Proposal: learn the communication topology. A small policy decides who talks to whom at each round, trained with RL on task success.
- Leo: Proposal: skip training entirely. Agents negotiate their roles in-context before solving, like a short planning meeting among themselves.
- Mina: Negotiation sounds cheap, but I'm not sure it adapts to anything beyond the first round.
- Leo: Learning a policy per task sounds expensive for a lab with four GPUs.
- Jae: Question — how do we even measure that self-organization helped? Success rate alone may hide the extra cost.
- Prof. Han: Good point. Both directions are interesting; I want a one-page proposal from each of you before we pick anything.

## Decisions
- Umbrella topic: self-organizing LLM agent teams, aiming for ICML 2027.
