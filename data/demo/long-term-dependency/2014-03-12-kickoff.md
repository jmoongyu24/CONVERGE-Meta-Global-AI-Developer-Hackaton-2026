# Kickoff — long-range dependencies

Date: 2014-03-12 (Wed) 10:00–11:30
Attendees: Prof. Ruiz, Daniel, Sora, Amir
Note-taker: Amir

## Problem
- Prof. Ruiz: Our document QA task needs facts from 200+ tokens back. The current model forgets almost everything after 15–20 steps.
- Prof. Ruiz: Baseline for now: a vanilla RNN language model, truncated BPTT of 35 steps, gradient clipping at norm 5.
- Daniel: Clipping fixes exploding gradients, not vanishing ones. The signal from 200 steps back is effectively zero by the time it arrives.
- Sora: Agreed — that is the Bengio et al. 1994 result, and Pascanu et al. 2013 show the same. A plain RNN will not get there no matter how we tune it.
- Amir: Confirmed on our data: accuracy is at chance for questions whose answer is more than 30 tokens back.
- (Everyone agreed: the vanilla RNN cannot carry information over long ranges; we need a different architecture, not more tuning.)

## Directions
- Daniel: I want to try LSTM: a gated memory cell that lets error flow back unchanged over many steps.
- Sora: Worth trying, but gated units are slow — four matrix multiplies per step, and we already wait days for training.
- Prof. Ruiz: Start with LSTM as the first serious attempt; keep the vanilla RNN as the baseline.
