# Weekly 2 — first hardware numbers

Date: 2026-03-18 (Wed), 13:00–14:20. Everyone present; notes by Marco.

Prof. Okafor opened by ruling out real-robot RL for this project: one arm, no spare force sensor, and no way to run it safely overnight. Hana agreed to drop it.

Yuki ran Hana's first domain-randomized policy on hardware with the new protocol (50 trials per object). Round pegs: 62% success. Square pegs: 18%. The policy jams at the chamfer; Yuki reads it as a contact-model mismatch rather than a perception problem and thinks calibrating the contact model is the more promising fix.

I argued this is exactly what randomization cannot fix: the simulator's contact model is wrong in a systematic way, and randomizing around a wrong model just spreads the error. Hana disagreed: the friction range was too narrow, and widening it plus randomizing the chamfer geometry should cover the square-peg case.

Prof. Okafor asked both of us for one experiment each that could prove the other wrong within two weeks.

Actions: Hana widens friction and chamfer randomization. I calibrate friction and contact stiffness from 20 real insertions. Yuki adds force traces to every hardware trial.
