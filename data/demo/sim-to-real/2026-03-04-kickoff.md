# Kickoff — sim-to-real for tight-tolerance insertion

Date: 2026-03-04 (Wed) 13:00–14:30
Attendees: Prof. Okafor, Hana, Marco, Yuki
Note-taker: Yuki

## Goal
- Prof. Okafor: Peg-in-hole insertion with 0.5 mm clearance on our single Franka arm. Policies are trained in simulation; the question is how to make them work on the real robot.
- Prof. Okafor: We have one arm and about 10 hours of robot time per week. Every method must respect that.

## Discussion
- Hana: Proposal: massive domain randomization. Randomize friction, mass, damping, peg geometry and camera pose so widely that the real world looks like one more variation.
- Marco: Proposal: the opposite direction, system identification. Calibrate the simulator's contact parameters from a few real rollouts so the sim matches our robot.
- Hana: Calibration fits one setup; as soon as we change the peg material it is wrong again.
- Marco: Randomizing everything makes the policy conservative; for 0.5 mm clearance you need precise contact behaviour, not average behaviour.
- Hana: Also floated: skip sim entirely and run RL directly on the real robot.
- Prof. Okafor: Real-robot RL worries me — wear on the arm and the force sensor, plus safety with only one robot.
- Yuki: Question — what counts as success on hardware? Simulated success rates have been misleading in past projects.
- (Everyone agreed: simulation metrics alone are not trustworthy; we only report hardware results.)
